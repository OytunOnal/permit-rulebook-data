# Excluded routes — and why

The dataset only models routes whose outcome a person can compute from facts they
can declare about themselves. Routes below were researched (2026-09-02 curation
pass) and deliberately left out. Each entry names the reason; "discretionary"
means an authority weighs evidence rather than checking declarable criteria.

## Germany (DE)

| Route | Reason |
|---|---|
| IT specialists (§ 19c Abs. 2 without qualification) | Covered by the modeled experienced-worker route (§ 6 BeschV IT path). |
| Western Balkans regulation (§ 26 Abs. 2 BeschV) | Nationality-restricted quota scheme with labour-agency discretion. Read at its own text (`https://www.buzer.de/26_BeschV.htm`, 2026-09-10): open to nationals of Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Kosovo, Montenegro, North Macedonia and Serbia for any employment with the labour agency's approval, but only where the application is filed at a German mission in one of those six states, capped at 50,000 approvals a calendar year, and refused to anyone who drew asylum-seeker benefits in the preceding 24 months. Not an exception to the modeled experienced-worker route — a different legal basis a person uses instead of it. |
| Employment of nationals of certain states (§ 26 Abs. 1 BeschV) | Read at its own text (`https://www.buzer.de/26_BeschV.htm`, 2026-09-10): nationals of Andorra, Australia, Israel, Japan, Canada, the Republic of Korea, Monaco, New Zealand, San Marino, the United Kingdom and the United States may be approved for **any** employment, with no qualification or degree required — the labour agency's own guidance says so in as many words (`https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/datei/dok_ba033210.pdf`, Stand 12/2024, read 2026-09-10). It is a way in this dataset does not model, not a condition lifted from a route it does: a national of one of those states who wants a modeled route still meets that route's rules. |
| Where an application may be filed (§ 41 AufenthV) | Deliberately excluded as procedure. Nationals of Australia, Israel, Japan, Canada, the Republic of Korea, New Zealand, the United Kingdom and the United States may enter without a visa and obtain the residence title inside Germany — and may not, if what they are applying for is the ICT Card (§ 41 Abs. 4). This dataset states the conditions a route asks of a person, not where the paperwork is done; the distinction is what keeps "criteria met" meaning the same thing on every route. Read 2026-09-10 at `https://www.buzer.de/41_AufenthV.htm`. |
| Freelance / self-employment (§ 21) | **Still not scored — quoted and dated since s9** as `de-selbstaendige-taetigkeit`. Business-plan viability assessment, and the statute says so itself: the decision turns on the viability of the idea, the applicant's entrepreneurial experience, the capital committed, the effect on jobs and training and the contribution to innovation and research, weighed with the local chambers, the trade authorities and the professional bodies (§ 21 Abs. 1 Sätze 2–3, read at `https://www.buzer.de/21_AufenthG.htm`, 2026-09-10). A page that states those rules is not a route this product scores; nothing on it is asked. |

## France (FR)

| Route | Reason |
|---|---|
| Carte « salarié » / « travailleur temporaire » (F15898) | **Still not scored — quoted and dated since s9** as `fr-salarie`. The card is mechanical, but the prerequisite autorisation de travail embeds a labour-market test plus employer-side compliance checks — not computable from applicant facts. Note for the next curator: CESEDA art. L414-13, which carries the opposabilité sentence in as many words, is on Legifrance, and **Legifrance answers this project's fetcher with 403** (measured 2026-09-10, over `codes/article_lc/…` as well as `ceta/…`). The quotes s9 ships therefore come from the two service-public fiches that do fetch — F15898 for the citizen's half and `entreprendre.service-public.gouv.fr` F2728 for the employer's — and those state the three-week advertisement, the employer's record and the disproportionate-recruitment refusal directly. **Three exemptions from the labour-market test are recorded here rather than on the page,** for the same reason: each lives only on Legifrance, which this fetcher cannot read — the 2008 Franco-Tunisian protocol's Annex I occupations (« sans que soit prise en compte la situation de l'emploi », point 2.3.3, décret n° 2009-905), master's graduates on a qualifying contract (CESEDA art. L421-4, which defers the figure to a décret and does not state it), and the narrower cases at Code du travail art. R5221-21. The first is worth a curator's eye: the ministry's own plain-language page about the Franco-Tunisian agreement does **not** carry that exemption, so a Tunisian reader following the overview would never meet it. |
| ~~Talent — entreprise innovante / salarié en mission~~ | **No longer excluded — modelled in s5c** as `fr-talent-innovante` and `fr-talent-mission`. The qualifier each subtype needed is now a declarable fact: `fr_innovative_employer` and `fr_local_contract`. Both carry the F16922 threshold (€39,582) with its own quote; the R&D link, the ministry recognition and the 3-month group seniority ship as preconditions, not as scored criteria. |
| Talent — professions médicales et de la pharmacie | Salary floor is fixed (€41,386.48) but eligibility hinges on an upstream health-code exercise authorization — a separate administrative act. |
| Talent — artistic professions / national renown / porteur de projet / mandataire social | Portfolio, reputation, business-plan or corporate-officer assessments — discretionary or not employment-based. |
| Talent — chercheur | The gate is the hosting agreement (convention d'accueil), like the modeled DE/ES/NL researcher routes — but France's fiche adds a master's-degree gate and no salary rule; candidate for a later modeling pass. |
| Travailleur saisonnier | Seasonal, ≤ 6 months/year, labour-market-tested. |
| Certificat de résidence d'1 an pour Algérien (`https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F2215`, read to the end 2026-09-10) | **What an Algerian national actually applies for, and it is not on this site.** France applies the accord franco-algérien du 27 décembre 1968 to Algerian nationals instead of the residence-permit provisions of its own code — *"les dispositions du code de l'entrée et du séjour des étrangers et du droit d'asile qui sont relatives aux différents titres de séjour … ne sont pas applicables aux ressortissants algériens"* (Conseil d'État, 2e et 7e ch. réunies, 2 mars 2026, n° 500835, `https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/ceta/id/CETATEXT000053612496`). The certificate is one permit with seventeen situations for a first application and sixteen for a renewal; the one a qualified job offer leads to is *"Vous voulez travailler comme salarié en France"*, and for it the page says *"Le certificat de résidence portera la mention salarié."* Not modelled as a route: its situations turn on a life already under way in France — years resided, a marriage, a child, a regroupement familial — which this interview never asks about, and the one employment situation carries the same opposable autorisation de travail that keeps the carte « salarié » out of the dataset (row above). Modelled instead where it decides something a reader can declare: `fr-ict` is closed to an Algerian passport, in the fiche's own words. |
| Whether an Algerian national may hold a passeport talent (searched 2026-09-10, **open**) | **Recorded as unsettled, not resolved.** The sources conflict and neither side reaches these four permits directly. For the agreement: the Conseil d'État sentence above — but that case concerned a certificat de résidence *"commerçant"*, so it settles the principle and not the talent cards; and the certificat de résidence page enumerates its situations to the end with no passeport talent and no carte bleue européenne among them (grepped over the whole fetched page, 2026-09-10 — zero occurrences of either term). Against it: Directive (EU) 2021/1883 art. 3(1), *"This Directive applies to third-country nationals who apply to be admitted, or who have been admitted, to the territory of a Member State for the purpose of highly qualified employment under this Directive."* (`https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32021L1883`). Searched on service-public, france-visas, the ministry and the ANEF portal on 2026-09-10 for a page that says which way it falls: none found. So `fr-talent-qualifie`, `fr-talent-blue-card`, `fr-talent-innovante` and `fr-talent-mission` are scored for an Algerian passport exactly as for any other non-EU passport, and the notice fr-dz-talent-open-question states the conflict on the results and on all four route pages. Hiding the routes would answer the question by omission; offering them silently would answer it the other way. |

## Spain (ES)

| Route | Reason |
|---|---|
| Nationality-specific rules, on all four Spanish routes | Read 2026-09-10 against the three `inclusion.gob.es` route pages, Ley 14/2013 arts. 61–76 in the BOE's consolidated text, and the salary-threshold PDF: **these routes ask the same of every third-country nationality.** No country is named anywhere in arts. 61–76. The one country-group carve-out is art. 61.2 — *"Lo dispuesto en esta sección no será de aplicación a los ciudadanos de la Unión Europea y a aquellos extranjeros que disfruten de derechos de libre circulación equivalentes"* — which is the free-movement gate this dataset already applies (its class holds Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein and Switzerland, checked the same day). Recorded so the silence is a finding with a date on it rather than a gap. |
| Investor "golden visa" (Ley 14/2013 arts. 63–67) | Abolished — BOE: "(Sin contenido)… con efectos de 3 de abril de 2025" (LO 1/2025). |
| General regime cuenta ajena (RD 1155/2024 arts. 72–81) | **Still not scored — quoted and dated since s9** as `es-cuenta-ajena`. Labour-market test (situación nacional de empleo): the catalogue path is deterministic but quarterly and per autonomous community; the non-catalogue path is the immigration office weighing the employer's own recruiting (art. 75.2). Read 2026-09-10 from the BOE's **consolidated HTML** at `https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2024-24099`, which carries arts. 73–76 in full. Correcting an assumption of research-04: the BOE's consolidated **PDF** is not usable here — this project's PDF decoder returns font and stream noise for it, not text. |

The CNO-2011 groups 1–2 limb stays out of the scoring — the SEPE catalogue URL is still unverified — and is stated on the card as a caveat, which since 2026-09-07 carries the UGE PDF's own wording of both limbs (`reduced-also-for-shortage-occupations`). One thing to note while it stays out: the PDF is narrower than the caveat's plain English. It offers the reduced threshold to "Ocupaciones de difícil cobertura incluidas en los grupos 1 y 2 de la CNO-2011", i.e. within the CNO's managerial and professional groups, where the caveat says only "Spain's official list of shortage occupations". The quote sits beside the sentence on the card, so a reader sees both; tightening the sentence is a candidate edit, not a verdict change (nothing scores off it). |
| EU Blue Card — whether the reduced threshold carries an institution restriction (2026-09-07) | **Settled 2026-09-07 — by reading the PDF, not by guessing.** s5d replaced the country-less `qualification_recent` gate on the three Dutch routes because the IND's own text puts an institution restriction behind it, and this row stood open because the Spanish source was assumed unreadable. It is not: the UGE salary PDF (edición Junio 2026) carries a text layer, and it states the limb — "Personas nacionales de terceros países que hayan obtenido la cualificación requerida en los tres años previos a la solicitud de la Tarjeta Azul-UE" — naming a qualification, a three-year window and no institution anywhere. `es-blue-card` therefore keeps `qualification_recent` unchanged: the wording backs what was already modelled rather than changing it, so no verdict moves. The card tells the reader as much in its own words, in the reading `salary-figures-and-their-limits-read-by-a-person`. The criterion carried an unsourced `note` saying the same thing until 2026-09-07, when `note` was removed as the one renderable slot that declared no kind (review). What is still unread is Orden PJC/44/2026 itself, which the ministry does publish as a scanned image; the PDF above is a restatement of it by the same ministry, not the Orden. |
| Digital nomad (arts. 74 bis–quinquies) | Employment-based but needs a new "remote employer elsewhere" situation, and the income floor is a formula rather than a figure: the binding instruction says only « cantidad que represente mensualmente el 200% del salario mínimo interprofesional (SMI) » (Instrucción conjunta, Tercera.1.a), and the SMI is reset by a separate decree each year. **Corrected 2026-09-10:** this row previously said no official euro figure exists to quote. Beside the arithmetic sits a judgement: « se podrá emplear cualquier medio de prueba y se efectuará un análisis individualizado » (Tercera.2). **Still not scored — quoted and dated since s9** as `es-teletrabajo`, from Ley 14/2013 arts. 61–62 and 74 bis–quinquies in the BOE's consolidated text. ## The twin a machine reads

Everything above is prose for a person. This block is the same facts in a shape
a test can read, and the two are held to each other: the route ids here must be
exactly the route ids the prose above names in backticks, and every limb id here
must be a statement or a reading the route actually carries and must appear in
that route's `scope.not_asked`. If the prose and this block disagree the build
fails, rather than letting a page name limbs nobody recorded.

One line per route this file records: the route id, then the dataset ids of the
limbs it records as left out or stated-but-not-asked. `(none)` is a real answer
— it means the file records this route in order to say that nothing of it is
excluded. A route that is quoted and not scored records **every** limb it
carries, because every limb of it is stated and none of it is asked (s9).
